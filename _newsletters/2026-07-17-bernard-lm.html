I trained a GPT on 15 years of my life — then interrogated it

A 10M-parameter language model, built from scratch on a MacBook Air from my Google search history and email, for $0 — and what cracking it open taught me about how AI decides who and what to believe in.

10.77Mparameters 67,792searches eaten 8,520reply pairs $0cost

This week I ran the entire modern LLM pipeline — pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, chat interface, interpretability probes — on a laptop, using my own Google Takeout as the corpus. The result schedules coffee at “14:30am,” believes Wednesday is the best day of the week, and reveals exactly how mention rates become AI beliefs. What's inside:

→ The 10× law. Model belief tracks the log of mentions (r = 0.95). Ten more mentions is noise; ten times more mentions is a new belief.

→ The Friday problem. My second-most-mentioned weekday is one the model refuses to recommend — because being talked about and being proposed are different flashcards, and models learn the difference.

→ The grep confession. My first “most-mentioned person” finding was destroyed by a word-boundary bug (“across” ≠ a guy named Ross). Why you should audit every entity-mention dashboard.

Plus the five-rule playbook for what actually moves an entity's weight in an AI model.
