The HIMS chase — buying a catalyst the market already owned

I bought 1,000 shares the night an FDA panel started voting yes on the peptides story I’d followed for months. By the next morning I was down 11% — on a day when every headline was the one the bulls wanted. A journal entry on what “priced in” actually means.

$33.03my entry 8–6–1the yes vote +58%run-up before it −11%next morning

On July 23 an FDA advisory committee voted to recommend BPC-157 — the best-known of seven peptides under review — for the compounding list Hims & Hers has been positioning around all year. I bought the news that evening. Friday the panel kept voting yes, the stock opened green… and then fell out of bed. Inside the post:

→ The finish-line effect. FDA put the date on the calendar in April; the stock ran +58% into the meeting. A dated catalyst gets bought for three months and sold at the tape-break — I bought the tape-break.

→ The yes that decided nothing. An advisory vote is non-binding, and FDA’s own staff had already published that none of the seven qualified. The best available headline got spent, so old money finally had its exit. Which was me.

→ My own scanner said AVOID that night. Extended 27% above the year’s average buyer, lagging the S&P all July. The rules worked; I overrode them for a story.

Plus the fix: a horizon-scanning agent now reads FDA calendars and Federal Register notices so the next runway gets caught in month one, not hour eleven — and the exact prices where this trade is over.
