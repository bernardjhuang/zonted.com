Trade Log 7/22: Three Open Theses and One Dead One

Bernard Huang
July 22, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR — trade log, July 22

Three open positions with written theses and tripwires — ABT, HOOD, V — and one thesis already disproven and cut: TSLA, bought for an earnings catalyst that didn’t show up.

  • TSLA: earnings play on an Optimus catalyst thesis. The print was bad. Sold out. Cost of a hypothesis test.
  • ABT: healthcare-plus-VWAP-reclaim long. The reclaim just survived its first retest; the “hot sector” half of the thesis is still pending.
  • HOOD: strongest structure in the book, 38 sessions above its earnings anchor. Everything gets re-underwritten at the July 29 print.
  • V: financials leadership with the deepest base — in profit against the year’s average buyer 100% of the last month. Reports July 28.

Writing these down because reviewing setups, entries, and exits is how the craft compounds. Levels and percentages only — the trading page stays quantity-free, and none of this is advice.

I’m starting a habit: every time the book changes, write down what I think I’m doing and why — then let the next few weeks grade the entry. The scan and the VWAP anchors on the trading page generate the signals; this log is the part only I can do, which is being honest about the thesis before the market makes me honest about it.

The framework, for anyone landing here first: I read every chart through two cost-basis anchors — the earnings VWAP (what the post-earnings cohort paid) and the YTD VWAP (what the whole year paid) — plus a spread z-score against SPY for relative strength. Above both anchors, every cohort is in profit and dips get defended. Below them, rallies meet trapped sellers. Entries want a hot sector, relative strength, and price above the anchors; exits are pre-committed to the same lines.

TSLA: the disproven one

Thesis at entry: a friend’s research made the case that Optimus progress would be the catalyst that turned this week’s earnings call bullish. I bought shares to play the print — a deliberate exception to my own no-entries-inside-the-earnings-window rule, made with eyes open: this was a bet on the binary, not despite it.

What happened: the results were bad, the catalyst didn’t materialize, and I sold out once that was clear. No averaging down, no “wait for the bounce.”

The review: the exit was the good part. Event trades have exactly one honest structure — a pre-stated catalyst, and an exit the moment the catalyst is disproven. That part worked. The entry deserves a harder look: the thesis lived entirely on someone else’s research, and I didn’t have an independent read on how much Optimus optimism was already in the price. When the only edge is borrowed, the position should be small and the exit fast. It was fast. Next time it should also be smaller.

ABT: half a thesis, freshly tested

Thesis at entry: healthcare is strengthening, and ABT broke back above both its earnings VWAP and its YTD VWAP after a clean Q2 report. Long shares, plus a January 2027 $120 call for the slower “January highs get revisited” version of the same idea.

Where it stands: the price half of the thesis is true and just got stress-tested — ABT dipped 22 basis points below the fresh earnings anchor for exactly one session, then closed back above it. A one-day undercut that gets bought back is how a level proves it’s real. The sector half is still pending: healthcare has been knocking on the hot-sector threshold for a week without crossing. Right now I’m long a fact and a forecast.

Tripwires, written down: three to five closes back under ∼100 means sellers control the quarter and the momentum leg is dead. A close through ∼99, the year’s anchor, kills the whole thesis — that’s under 2% away, which is the point: early entry, cheap invalidation. No earnings until October, so this one gets months to develop without a binary event. If the sector confirms, the framework says that’s when size goes from starter to full — not before.

HOOD: strong tape, referendum next week

Thesis at entry: financials are the hottest sector on the board, HOOD is a growth name inside it, and Robinhood Chain is accruing fees fast — onchain and agentic-commerce tailwinds that the market hasn’t fully priced. Shares plus a January 2027 $125 call for the long version.

Where it stands: the structure is the best in the book — 38 straight sessions above the earnings anchor with a 16% cushion, and the year’s buyers comfortably in profit. The sector premise is simply true. Two honest caveats I want on the record: relative strength is flat — HOOD is pacing the market, not leading it — and the broad crypto complex is not confirming (bitcoin and the majors are all underwater against their own yearly anchors on my crypto tab). So the thesis survives only in its specific form: chain fees as idiosyncratic revenue, not beta to coin prices.

The gate: earnings, July 29. The print is the referendum on the fee-growth claim. The cushion buys room for a wobble; it doesn’t buy immunity from a bad answer.

V: the boring one that works

Thesis at entry: financials sector leadership, expressed through the most liquid quality name in it. Shares plus a January 2027 $400 call — a deliberately boring instrument for a deliberately boring thesis.

Where it stands: deepest base in the book. Twenty-four sessions above the earnings anchor, 6.5% over it, and in profit against the year’s average buyer every single day of the last month. It slipped out of the scan’s ENTER tier this week when its spread pulled back with the tape — a pause, not a break. Invalidation is far away, which is what durability looks like.

The near event: reports July 28, the day before HOOD. Which brings up the real finding of writing all this down…

What I’m taking away

  • Two of my three open theses are the same thesis. HOOD and V are both, in my own words, “financials are hot.” Four of six instruments ride one sector premise. That’s a conviction bet expressed twice — fine if I size it as one thesis, dangerous if I pretend it’s diversification. ABT is the actual diversifier, and it’s the youngest, most fragile position.
  • The book has a scheduled 48-hour stress test: V prints July 28, HOOD July 29 — back-to-back events on the two positions that share a premise. Deciding now what I do with each outcome beats deciding at 4:05pm that day.
  • The TSLA exit is the habit I want to keep. Catalyst disproven, position gone, same week. The lesson isn’t “don’t play earnings” — it’s that borrowed conviction gets starter size and a hair trigger.
  • Fresh regimes get starter size; confirmation earns the add. ABT is a week-old regime; V is a month-old one. They should not be sized the same, and writing this down is how I catch myself when they drift that way.

That’s the log. The next entry writes itself next week — two earnings prints will grade two of these theses, and the sector gauge will tell me whether ABT’s forecast half becomes fact. Positions and levels stay published on the trading page; nothing here is investment advice, and a logged trade is not a recommendation — it’s me showing my work.
