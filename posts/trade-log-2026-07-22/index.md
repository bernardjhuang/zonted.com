New rule: every time I put on a trade, I write down the thesis and invalidation levels in plain English, then let the tape grade my homework over the next few weeks. This is entry one.

Three open positions — ABT, HOOD, and V — with exact entry rationales, anchored-VWAP levels, and pre-committed stops.

**How to read the charts below.** Every chart tracks two anchored volume-weighted average prices (VWAPs). The **purple line** is the average price paid *since the last earnings report*. The **gray dashed line** is the average price paid *year-to-date*. When a stock trades above both lines, the average holder is in profit and dips get bought. Below them, the average holder is underwater and bounces run into breakeven supply. Above the lines is a tailwind; below is a headwind.

## ABT: half a thesis, freshly tested

**Why I bought:** Two reasons: healthcare looked ready to rotate into leadership, and ABT reclaimed both anchored VWAPs after a clean July 16 earnings print. I bought common shares plus a January 2027 call option for longer-dated convexity.

*ABT this year. Fell hard, based out, then jumped back above both lines after the July 16 earnings report. The purple line just formed — this trend is only a week old.*

**Where it stands:** The stock-specific half of the thesis is working. ABT undercut the purple post-earnings line for a single session this week and buyers immediately defended it. The sector half, however, is still lagging — healthcare (XLV) keeps flirting with leadership without breaking through:

*The healthcare sector fund (XLV) vs its year-average price. Recovering, recently back above the dashed line — but not yet a leader. My “healthcare is hot” premise is still a forecast, not a fact.*

**Pre-committed exits:** If ABT spends roughly a week back under $100, the post-earnings momentum thesis has failed and I cut the shares. If it closes below ~$99 — the year-to-date VWAP — the entire trade is dead, no debate. Those stops sit less than 2% below spot, which is the whole point of entering near the reclaim: finding out I’m wrong is cheap. With no earnings until October, the position has months of clean runway.

## HOOD: strong chart, big test next week

**Why I bought:** Financials lead the tape right now, and Robinhood is the high-beta growth engine inside that group. On top of core brokerage momentum, their blockchain business is compounding fee volume fast, giving the stock upside to the onchain and agentic-commerce buildout. Long shares plus a January 2027 call.

*Robinhood Chain daily fees since its May launch: from roughly nothing to about $250K per day. Data: [DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/fees/chain/robinhood-chain). This is the “collecting fees fast” part of the thesis, in one picture.*

*HOOD this year. Above the purple line for 38 straight trading days, sitting about 16% above it. Most owners are comfortably in profit.*

**Where it stands:** The healthiest chart I own, with two honest caveats. First, HOOD is tracking the broader market rather than outpacing it — for a high-beta leader in a hot sector, I want to see genuine relative strength. Second, spot crypto prices have had a sluggish year, so Robinhood’s fee growth has to stand on its own execution rather than a retail crypto mania.

**The test:** Earnings arrive July 29. That print either validates the fee-growth trajectory or breaks it. A 16% cushion above the year-to-date VWAP buys room to absorb noise, not immunity from a thesis miss.

## V: the boring one that works

**Why I bought:** The same sector thesis as HOOD — financials leading the market — expressed through the steadiest compounder in the index. Visa simply collects a toll on global payment volume. Long shares plus a January 2027 call.

*V this year. Above the purple line for 24 days and above the year-average line every single day for the past month. Nothing dramatic ever happens on this chart. That’s the appeal.*

*The financials sector fund (XLF) vs its year-average price. Comfortably above the dashed line — this is what a genuinely strong sector looks like, and it’s the engine behind both HOOD and V.*

**Where it stands:** The deepest, calmest uptrend in my book. It pulled back slightly with the index this week — a routine pause above both VWAPs. Reports July 28, one day before HOOD.

## What I’m taking away

- **I really have two bets on, not three.** HOOD and V express the exact same macro view — financials stay in leadership — in different beta wrappers. Pretending they diversify each other would be lying to myself; they have to be sized as a single correlated exposure.
- **Next week grades the book in 48 hours.** V reports July 28; HOOD follows July 29. Defining my post-earnings reaction rules *before* the prints keeps me from making emotional decisions at 4:05 p.m. while staring at a red print.
- **New trends get starter sizing; proven trends earn size.** ABT’s reclaim is a week old; V’s uptrend is more than a month old. Writing the log forces me to keep position sizing aligned with trend maturity.
