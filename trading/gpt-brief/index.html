CAPR Deramiocel for Duchenne cardiomyopathy — July 29 AdCom · August 22 PDUFA Tier 1

Trigger. FDA's cell-and-gene-therapy committee votes July 29 after reviewing BLA 125842; the final action date is August 22. The committee packet is due no later than two business days before the meeting.

Why mispriced. The clinical headline is positive, but the regulatory event is actually two-dimensional: efficacy and the manufacturability of an allogeneic cell product. Base case. The committee acknowledges efficacy but splits on durability, endpoint interpretation, or CMC. FDA either approves with a narrower label/post-marketing burden or delays for additional manufacturing commitments.

Invalidation. FDA postpones the meeting or extends the action date; either change breaks the current timing and makes the quoted option bracket stale.

Trigger. FDA's cell-and-gene-therapy committee reviews BLA 125827 on July 30, followed by the August 2 action date. This is a repeat regulatory attempt after prior Complete Response Letters.

Why mispriced. The same 34% response statistic supports both narratives: extraordinary activity in refractory melanoma and selection/attribution risk in a single-arm study. A repeated-review setup also makes precedent less useful than the actual July 30 voting question.

Base case. A divided committee: durable responses look real, but FDA demands tighter evidence on contribution and control selection. A narrow approval, delayed action, or onerous confirmatory requirement produces a less explosive outcome.

Invalidation. FDA delays the committee or action date, or publishes briefing documents showing the vote is not on approvability/contribution.

Invalidation. The company moves the mission beyond September 5 or separates the three satellites into a different manifest; either breaks the current event definition and option expiry match.
