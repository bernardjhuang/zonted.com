## 1. Houthi tanker strikes + Trump threatens Iran — oil shipping disruption risk

What happened: Houthi forces hit two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea overnight. President Trump publicly stated Iran will be held "responsible" and threatened "major military response." This raises the risk of disruptions to the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb chokepoints — through which ~20% of global oil and LNG flows.

Why it matters for markets: If tanker traffic is disrupted or insurance premiums spike, the direct beneficiaries are:

- Crude tanker companies (rates jump on longer routes and risk premium)

- Oil/energy prices (supply shock premium)

- Defense contractors (military escalation spending)

## 2. Oil spike reopening the door to a July Fed hike

What happened: The oil gap (+~9% on WTI) is pushing inflation expectations back up at exactly the wrong time — one week before the FOMC meeting. Polymarket odds of a 25bp hike in July jumped from ~12% to ~22% in 24 hours. If the Fed is forced to hike into a geopolitical shock, that's a materially different risk environment.

Why it matters for markets: Duration assets (long bonds, REITs, utilities) and rate-sensitive financials move first. If hike odds keep climbing:

- Long Treasury yields grind higher (TLT sells off)

- Regional banks and financials get bid (wider net interest margins)

- High-duration equities (growth/tech) face multiple compression
