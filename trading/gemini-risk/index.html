Current market stance

Neutral to Risk-Off

Mixed equities, rising volatility, Middle East energy risk, new tariffs, and the possibility of renewed inflation leave the market defensive without reaching panic.

4/ 10risk appetite

Assessment 2026-07-26 · market data through 2026-07-24

01

Equities — S&P 500 and Nasdaq

The stock market has been mixed, with a noticeable rotation out of riskier assets. The S&P 500 has struggled to find footing after geopolitical shocks, while the tech-heavy Nasdaq has shown distinct weakness.

Blue chips and mid-caps have shown enough resilience to prevent a full-blown selloff, but momentum is strained.

02

Volatility — VIX

The VIX has trended higher into the 18.5–19 range, versus roughly 17.9 a month earlier. That is elevated anxiety, not panic.

Commodity volatility is more severe: oil volatility is roughly three times the VIX as Middle East tensions lift energy risk.

03

Geopolitical and macro risks

Geopolitics are the largest immediate headwind. The collapse of the US–Iran ceasefire and renewed disruption around the Strait of Hormuz have pushed equities lower and energy risk premiums higher.

Markets are also absorbing new global US tariffs in the 10%–12.5% range, replacing temporary levies and adding uncertainty to global trade.

04

Inflation — CPI

June 2026 headline CPI slowed to 3.5% year over year from 4.2% in May, while core inflation eased to 2.6%.

That disinflation is positive, but the oil-price shock threatens to reaccelerate inflation in coming months and keeps investors cautious.

05

Federal Reserve and rates

The Federal Reserve is expected to hold its benchmark rate at 3.50%–3.75% at the July 28–29 meeting.

Rising energy prices have increased concern that inflation could force another hike before year-end. The prospect of higher-for-longer rates continues to weigh on growth stocks.

Bottom line

The market sits at 4/10, leaning risk-off. This is not capitulation: broad selling has not reached panic levels. But active military conflict threatening oil routes, aggressive tariff policy, weak technology momentum, and the risk of reaccelerating inflation have killed near-term exuberance. Capital is playing defense while it waits for clarity on energy prices and Fed policy.
