2026-08-03 · Risk-on (7/10)

Oil crash and broad equity rally trigger Risk-on: every index green, crude down 5%, yields ease as inflation overhang cracks

US equities delivered a decisive broad-based rally on 2026-08-03 to open August: SPY +1.42% near 757.63, QQQ +1.75% near 700.06, DIA +1.32% near 531.24, IWM +1.72% near 296.22. The index-level gains were confirmed by Yahoo Finance reporting S&P 500 at 7,600.50 (+1.48%), Nasdaq at 25,913.90 (+2.13%), Dow at 53,178.41 (+1.32%), and Russell 2000 at 2,982.15 (+1.73%). Every major index rose at least 1.3% with small caps fully participating — the breadth signature that was absent throughout most of July's fractured tape.

The dominant cross-asset signal was a massive crude oil selloff: WTI fell 5.30% to $80.18 and Brent dropped 4.75% to $83.75, with USO plunging 5.43% to 122.16. This is the largest single-session decline in the current Hormuz crisis window and effectively reverses a significant portion of the geopolitical risk premium that had been building through July. Whether driven by de-escalation signals, demand-destruction fears at these prices, or OPEC+ signaling, the market unambiguously interpreted lower oil as inflation-relief positive: equities rallied, the dollar was flat (DXY +0.02%), and breakeven rates implicitly compressed.

Treasury yields pulled back modestly in sympathy: the 10Y fell 1.24% to 4.686% and the 30Y dropped 0.83% to 5.23%, while the 5Y declined 1.35% to 4.40%. TLT was roughly flat (-0.06% to 82.20) as the long-end bond rally was contained — yields remain elevated in absolute terms with the 30Y still above 5.2%, but the directionality cooperated with risk assets today. The 13-week T-bill yield ticked up 0.49% to 3.70%, keeping the short end anchored.

Mega-cap leadership was constructive: Amazon extended Friday's 15% AWS blowout with a further +4.63% to $284.16, NVIDIA rose 2.93% to $206.64, and the semiconductor complex held gains. Apple was the notable laggard, declining 1.85% to $303.21 on continued selling pressure from last Thursday's Tim Cook services-growth warning. SOFI (+10.55%) and SNAP (+7.36%) showed broad risk appetite extending beyond mega-caps. The VIX fell 1.50% to 15.75, confirming reduced demand for portfolio protection.

International markets were mixed but mostly constructive: Europe rallied sharply (DAX +1.45%, CAC 40 +1.22%, EURO STOXX 50 +1.08%) while Asia was more bifurcated — the KOSPI plunged 5.12% (giving back much of last week's AI-driven 14% surge on profit-taking), the Nikkei fell 0.94%, but the Hang Seng rose 0.48% and India's BSE SENSEX gained 0.70%. Gold was flat (GLD +0.05%, futures +0.02% to $4,107.70), silver rose 0.75%, and copper gained 1.25% — the industrial metal strength aligning with the risk-on tone.

What Supports Risk

  • The 5%+ crash in crude oil is the single most bullish macro development in weeks: it directly relieves the inflation pressure that kept the Fed hawkish and consumer sentiment fragile throughout July, and the market is pricing it as supply-side relief rather than demand destruction given the synchronous equity rally.
  • Broad-based equity participation with all four major ETFs (SPY, QQQ, DIA, IWM) rising 1.3-1.8% in lockstep addresses the breadth problem flagged in the July 31 journal — today's rally was not concentrated in a single mega-cap name.
  • Amazon's follow-through (+4.63% after Friday's 15% surge) confirms the AWS earnings thesis has legs beyond a one-day squeeze, and NVDA's +2.93% shows the AI complex maintaining momentum into August.
  • The VIX declining to 15.75 and the steady intraday accumulation pattern in SPY (no drawdowns below the opening level) signal genuine buying pressure rather than a short-covering spike.
  • Treasury yields cooperated by falling across the curve (10Y -1.2%, 30Y -0.8%, 5Y -1.4%), creating the classic risk-on cross-asset configuration: stocks up, bonds up (yields down), oil down, VIX down.

What Holds It Back

  • The 30-year Treasury yield remains at 5.23% — still near multi-decade highs in absolute terms — and one session of modest easing does not reverse the structural bond-vigilante pressure that built under Chair Warsh through July.
  • Apple's -1.85% decline shows that not all mega-caps are participating, and the Tim Cook services-growth warning from last week continues to weigh on the most heavily weighted stock in the S&P 500.
  • The KOSPI's -5.12% plunge reveals that last week's euphoric Asia chip rally (South Korea +14% in one session) was speculative excess now being unwound, which could spread to other crowded AI-trade positions.
  • One strong session does not reverse July's structural damage: the single-stock blowups (Reddit -21%, Roblox -27%), the reported $45B hedge fund blowup, and the three FOMC hike dissents are still in the rearview mirror.
  • It is unclear whether the oil crash reflects positive supply news (Hormuz de-escalation) or negative demand news (growth slowing) — if the latter, the equity rally may be mispricing the signal.

What Changes My Mind

  • If tomorrow's session confirms the rally with a second consecutive broad advance and the 30Y yield breaks back below 5.0%, the regime would solidify as Risk-on with room to move toward 8-9.
  • Concrete news of Hormuz reopening or an Iran ceasefire framework would validate the oil crash as a lasting positive supply shock rather than a one-day repricing, unlocking further risk appetite.
  • Conversely, if oil rebounds sharply (mean-reverting the 5% drop) and yields back up, today's rally would look like a head-fake and the score would revert toward Neutral.
  • Apple stabilizing and small-cap breadth persisting (rather than IWM fading after a one-day pop) would confirm the breadth improvement is structural rather than opportunistic.
