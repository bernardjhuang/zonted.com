2026-08-07 · Risk-on (7/10)

Broad equity rally (SPY +0.60%, QQQ +1.17%, IWM +1.10%) with cooperative yields and weak dollar lifts risk appetite to 7, but gold surges to $4,400 and oil climbs a second day

US equities staged a decisive broad-based rally on 2026-08-07, recovering from two consecutive sessions of decline. The S&P 500 closed at 7,743.77 (+0.44%), Nasdaq at 26,603.66 (+0.97%), Dow at 53,946.29 (+0.11%), and Russell 2000 at 3,031.45 (+1.00%). In ETF terms: SPY +0.60% to 773.20, QQQ +1.17% to 723.04, DIA +0.27% to 539.62, IWM +1.10% to 301.53. The rally's composition is constructive: the Nasdaq and Russell 2000 led while the Dow lagged, the exact inverse of yesterday's risk-off pattern where the Dow's -0.85% decline signaled defensive rotation. Small-cap leadership (IWM +1.10%) is especially significant because it was the weakest performer yesterday (-0.51%), confirming that institutional money is rotating back into cyclical and growth names. The Dow's minimal gain (+0.11%) reflects the ongoing rotation away from defensive value, which is consistent with a risk-on regime even if it caps the headline index.

Treasury yields resumed their cooperative easing trajectory after yesterday's bearish backup. The 10Y fell -0.39% to 4.652%, the 5Y dropped -0.77% to 4.355%, and the 13-week T-bill eased -0.38% to 3.718%. TLT gained +0.27% to 82.75. The 30Y, however, held at 5.21% (-0.13%) — still above the 5.2% threshold that yesterday's journal flagged as the key condition for the risk-on case. The yield curve's bear-flattening dynamic (short and belly yields falling more than the long end) is a mixed signal: it suggests the market is pricing Fed easing (bullish for risk) but remains cautious about long-term inflation/fiscal risk (the 30Y refusal to break below 5.2%). This is the primary technical cap on the risk score.

The session's most extraordinary development is gold's surge to $4,399.80 (+2.33%), following Tuesday's +3.74% explosion. GLD gained +2.26% to 398.49. Silver surged +3.20% to $63.58 and platinum rose +1.44% to $1,762.90. Gold has now risen approximately $130 over two trading sessions and sits just below the psychologically critical $4,400 level (spot price touched it intraday). This is one of the most powerful gold moves of the cycle and defies the risk-on equity narrative. Two interpretations: (a) the market is pricing a Fed pivot/cutting cycle that would lower real rates, benefiting non-yielding assets — this is bullish for equities too and represents a reflation trade; (b) institutional money is hedging against a systemic risk (geopolitical escalation, sovereign debt concern) that equities are not pricing — this is a warning. The simultaneous oil recovery (WTI +1.05% to $78.10, second consecutive up day) lends support to interpretation (a) — if inflation expectations are rising, both gold and oil should rise together. But copper's -1.70% decline to $6.59 directly contradicts the reflation narrative, since copper should benefit from growth/inflation. The copper-gold divergence is the most puzzling cross-asset signal of the session.

The VIX declined to 14.91 (-1.58%), making a new cycle low. Unlike yesterday — when the VIX fell amid unanimous cross-asset deterioration and was flagged as a complacency signal — today's VIX decline is more coherent with the risk-on tape: equities rallied, yields eased, and the dollar weakened. The options market is pricing very little near-term downside risk, and today's price action validated that positioning. However, the VIX at sub-15 levels while gold surges $100+ is a divergence worth monitoring. If gold continues higher on Monday and equities give back today's gains, the VIX would be forced to catch up rapidly.

International markets presented a mixed picture. Europe was solidly green: DAX +0.69%, EURO STOXX 50 +0.33%, FTSE 100 +0.31%, CAC 40 +0.17%. China surged with the SSE Composite +1.02%. But Asia ex-China remained stressed: KOSPI -0.60% (third consecutive decline), Nikkei -0.12%, India's BSE SENSEX -0.58%. The KOSPI's three-day losing streak (cumulative -5.25% from the Aug 4 close) is the most persistent EM stress signal in the current tape. The fact that China (SSE +1.02%) diverged positively from Korea and India suggests the stress is targeted at specific EMs rather than a global risk-off event. Brazil's IBOVESPA -1.67% adds to the EM stress picture.

What Supports Risk

  • The equity rally is broad and well-led: Nasdaq +0.97% and Russell 2000 +1.00% leading, with S&P 500 +0.44% — tech and small-caps leading is textbook risk-on. The Russell 2000's +1.10% recovery from yesterday's -0.51% decline confirms institutional money is rotating back into high-beta, cyclical, and small-cap names.
  • Treasury yields eased cooperatively: 10Y -0.39% to 4.652%, 5Y -0.77% to 4.355%, 13Wk -0.38% to 3.718% — the easing trajectory resumed after yesterday's one-day backup, removing the yield pressure that drove the Neutral 5 call. TLT +0.27% confirms bond investors are positioning for lower rates.
  • The dollar weakened broadly: DXY -0.37% to 99.56, with EUR/USD +0.36%, USD/JPY -0.58%, USD/CAD -0.54%, USD/AUD -0.56% — every major dollar pair moved in the risk-on direction, confirming the currency market's alignment with the equity rally.
  • European equities were uniformly positive: DAX +0.69%, EURO STOXX 50 +0.33%, FTSE 100 +0.31%, CAC 40 +0.17% — the global risk-on tone extends beyond the US, with Europe confirming the move.
  • The VIX at 14.91 (-1.58%) is coherent with today's risk-on tape: equities up, yields down, dollar down. Unlike yesterday's complacency flag, the low VIX is now backed by supportive cross-asset price action, making it a valid risk-on confirmation.
  • Mega-cap tech held firm: NVDA +1.49% to $222.25, PLTR +9.56% to $170.82 — the AI/defense complex is providing anchor support for the broader market, and the concentration of gains in high-beta names (PLTR, SPCX +11.99%) shows speculative appetite remains healthy.
  • BTC +0.72% and ETH +0.47% are modestly positive, providing non-contradictory context for the risk-on equity tone.

What Holds It Back

  • Gold's extraordinary surge to $4,399.80 (+2.33%) — approaching the psychologically critical $4,400 level — is the single most concerning signal in an otherwise risk-on tape. With silver +3.20% and platinum +1.44%, the entire precious metals complex is bid. Gold has risen ~$130 in two sessions. If this is a flight-to-quality signal that equities are ignoring, it represents a major divergence that historically resolves in gold's favor.
  • The 30Y Treasury yield held at 5.21% (-0.13%), remaining above the 5.2% threshold that yesterday's journal flagged as the key condition. While the short end and belly eased, the long end's refusal to break below 5.2% signals persistent long-term inflation or fiscal concern. The yield curve is bear-flattening in a way that caps equity valuation expansion.
  • Copper declined -1.70% to $6.59 — the industrial metal most correlated with global growth diverged negatively from a risk-on equity rally. If the equity market is pricing cyclical recovery, copper should confirm. Its decline suggests either supply-side quirks or genuine demand weakness that equities are ignoring.
  • Oil's continued recovery (WTI +1.05% to $78.10, Brent +1.12% to $83.41) is the second consecutive up day, partially unwinding the disinflationary catalyst that powered the Aug 3-4 rally. The two-day cumulative crude recovery (WTI +3.34% then +1.05% = ~+4.4%) retraces roughly 40% of the Aug 3-4 crash. If WTI reclaims $80+, the disinflationary narrative is in serious trouble.
  • The KOSPI's third consecutive decline (-0.60%) and Brazil's IBOVESPA -1.67% confirm persistent EM stress despite the US risk-on rally. Emerging markets lead the global manufacturing cycle, and sustained EM weakness has historically been a leading indicator for developed-market equity corrections.
  • The Dow's minimal gain (+0.11% to 53,946) vs. SPY +0.60% and QQQ +1.17% continues the pattern of narrow leadership. The Dow's 61-point gain is barely a rounding error, and its persistent underperformance suggests that institutional money remains selective rather than broadly risk-on.
  • The Trade Desk's -21.51% collapse on earnings is a cautionary signal: it demonstrates that beneath the index-level rally, earnings risk remains severe. Selective stock dispersion (DOCS +40.49% vs. TTD -21.51%) is a feature of late-cycle markets where index gains mask underlying fragility.

What Changes My Mind

  • If gold breaks above $4,500 on Monday, the flight-to-quality interpretation would be confirmed and the stance would shift to Risk-off at 3-4, regardless of equity price action. Conversely, if gold gives back $50+ and breaks below $4,350, the surge would be confirmed as a speculative blow-off and the risk score could rise to 8.
  • The 30Y yield is the linchpin: a break below 5.15% would signal the bond market has fully re-engaged the easing trajectory and would support a Risk-on 8 score. A move back above 5.25% would confirm the bear-steepening and likely trigger an equity selloff, dropping the score to 4-5.
  • Oil at the $80 level: if WTI reclaims $80 next week, the disinflationary narrative powering the equity rally collapses. If WTI falls back below $76, the crude rebound is confirmed as a two-day mean-reversion and the risk-on case strengthens.
  • Copper's direction on Monday: if copper rebounds alongside equities, the industrial demand signal is restored and the risk-on case is broad-based. A further decline below $6.40 would confirm industrial weakness and narrow the equity rally's foundation.
  • The KOSPI's fourth session: another decline would establish a four-day losing streak and signal deeper structural EM stress. A rebound would suggest the three-day decline was localized digestion after the Aug 3-4 surge.
